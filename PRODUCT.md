# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Stack

Next.js (user's choice), static export. No CMS — content lives in typed data files in the repo so entries are added by editing code.

## Users

Primary: other vintage van people — owners, restorers, parts hunters, and the merely curious — who arrive from a forum link, a search for a specific part or year, or a photo someone shared. They are looking at this on a phone as often as a laptop, often mid-scroll, and they came to see the van and find out what was actually done to it.

Secondary: the owner himself, using the site as the durable record of the work — dates, part numbers, what failed, what worked.

## Product Purpose

groovan.org is one person's public record of restoring two vintage vans. It exists so the work is documented rather than forgotten: what the vans are, where they came from, what has been done, what parts went in, and what is left. Success is a visitor who wanted to know something specific about these vans leaving with the answer, and a visitor who wanted to look at them staying to scroll.

## Positioning

Not a shop and not a forum. It is a first-person account of two specific vehicles with real dates, real parts, and real failures — the detail a general van-culture site or a marketplace listing cannot carry, because it belongs to these two vans only.

## Operating Context

- Work happens in a corrugated-metal shed with dirt floor and doors that swing open — not a professional bay.
- Progress is irregular: weekends, waiting on parts, seasons.
- Content arrives as phone photos plus notes; entries are dated and additive, never revised away.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Static site. No accounts, no comments, no forms wired to a backend at launch.
- Content is entered by editing repo files.
- Two vans:
  - **The Chevy** — 1970s Chevrolet G-series shorty van, finished. Custom sunburst livery: golden yellow, orange, rust, dark chocolate brown over cream, with slotted mag wheels and Firestone raised-white-letter tires.
  - **The Econoline** — Ford Econoline, yellow over brown two-tone, rear-mounted covered spare, `GROOVAN` plate. Currently in the shed, in progress.
- **Undecided / not yet supplied:** model years, engines, drivetrain specs, VINs, acquisition dates, mileage, part numbers, the owner's name, and any contact address. Every one of these ships as a clearly marked placeholder and must not be invented as fact.

## Brand Commitments

- Name: **groovan.org**. The `GROOVAN` license plate on the Econoline is real and is the name's source.
- Binding visual constraint from the user: the palette is taken from the two supplied photos — golden yellow, orange, rust/burnt sienna, dark chocolate brown, cream/tan body, plus the shed's corrugated metal and its faded teal and oxide-red.

## Evidence on Hand

- Two real photographs, supplied by the user and not yet on disk:
  1. The Chevy, side profile, street, sunburst stripe livery.
  2. The Econoline, rear three-quarter, inside the open shed, `GROOVAN` plate visible.
- Nothing else. No other photos, no written history, no spec sheet, no parts receipts, no third-party coverage. Future work must not fabricate any of it.

## Product Principles

1. **The vans lead.** Every page earns its place by showing or explaining an actual vehicle.
2. **Dated and additive.** The log is a record, not a marketing narrative; older entries stay as written.
3. **Specific over general.** A part number beats an adjective. Where the fact is unknown, say unknown.
4. **Nothing invented.** Unsupplied specs, dates, and history appear as visible blanks the owner fills, never as plausible fiction.
5. **Readable in a shed.** Large type, high contrast, works on a phone in daylight with dirty hands.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No product-specific requirement established beyond the general standard: WCAG 2.1 AA contrast, keyboard operability, and reduced-motion support.
